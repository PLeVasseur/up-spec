[[lola]]
== LoLa

NOTE: LoLa is the `+mw::com+` communication middleware of Eclipse S-CORE, in the repository https://github.com/eclipse-score/communication[`eclipse-score/communication`].

* *One revision.*
The facts that this page states about LoLa are taken from LoLa's own published documents at commit `72fe936e4d1e091181134ebdb8dbfed7f6574dc9` of that repository, and describe that revision.
The binding's qualification record states the revision of LoLa that the binding is built against, and governs for it (xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).
* *Facts and duties.*
A fact about LoLa is what those documents say.
It is no duty of the binding and no promise of this specification: the duties are those that the items state.
* *Qualification.*
LoLa's README states that the safety qualification of its elements is not provided (https://github.com/eclipse-score/communication/blob/72fe936e4d1e091181134ebdb8dbfed7f6574dc9/README.md[`+README.md+` at that commit], section Safety qualification).

[[lola-assumptions]]
=== LoLa's published assumptions of use

LoLa publishes assumptions of use for whoever uses it, and the binding is such a user.
The table lists some of them, each under LoLa's own name for it in https://github.com/eclipse-score/communication/blob/72fe936e4d1e091181134ebdb8dbfed7f6574dc9/score/mw/com/dependability/safety_analysis/aou.trlc[`+aou.trlc+` at that commit], and names what it meets here.
The second column says in this page's words what the assumption states, and for some what LoLa's note to it adds.
LoLa's own text governs what an assumption means: the last column says what the binding does about it.
The table is no list of all that LoLa publishes: <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>> requires the binding to keep, or to name who keeps, every one of LoLa's assumptions of use that applies to it.

|===
| LoLa's assumption | What it states, in this page's words | What it meets here

| `+OneProducerOnlyOneAllocateePtr+`
| At any time, a producer instance is to hold only one loan for each event
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>>, "An assumption that limits a route"; <<req-ssr-binding-lola-capacity,On LoLa, a route's counts are declared and its storage is established at setup>>

| `+NoCopySendWhileHoldingAllocateePtr+`
| `+Send(const& value)+` is not to be invoked while a loan is held

| `+CheckForAllocationError+`

| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>>, "An error that LoLa calls non-recoverable"; xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-application-reacts[The application reacts to what the element reports]

| `+Unsubscribe()+` is not to be called on a proxy event while any sample that it provided is still held, and the proxy of that proxy event is to be kept alive, because destroying the proxy would unsubscribe. | <<req-ssr-binding-lola-sample-lifetime,On LoLa, no subscription ends while one of its samples is held>>

| `+SkeletonAliveWhileItsAllocateePtrBeingUsed+`
| A skeleton is to be kept alive while any loan that it returned is used. LoLa's note gives the reason: destroying the skeleton invalidates the memory that such a loan points to
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>>, "What the binding keeps itself"; xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held], "A transmit loan"

| `+ValidityOfPointerOnLoLaPointer+`
| No pointer into the memory of a loan or of a received sample is to be used once the loan or the sample is no longer valid. LoLa's note: such memory is then unmanaged, and any access to it is undefined behaviour
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-lifetime,On LoLa, a loan exposes only its payload, and a receive view lives within its sample>>; xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]

| `+NoneTerminatingCallbacks+`
| No callback that is passed to LoLa for invocation is to throw. LoLa's note: a callback that throws makes the behaviour undefined
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-foreign-boundary,Nothing unwinds across the LoLa binding's boundary, and a termination path is a panic path>>, "The boundary"

| `+ValidCallbacksWhileProxyAlive+`
| Every callback that is passed to LoLa is to be valid for as long as its proxy is alive
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-callbacks,What the LoLa binding gives LoLa stays valid for as long as LoLa can use it>>. The assumption is a minimum: it says for how long a callback stays valid at least, and it names no point after which LoLa no longer uses one

| `+NoStaticContextSupport+`
| LoLa is not to be used in a static context in {cpp}
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>>, "What the binding keeps itself": the assumption is about the binding's own {cpp} code. It says nothing about how long a callback is used, and nothing about what LoLa's threads do when a process ends

| `+NoGuaranteeOnExecutionTime+`
| No execution time is guaranteed for any function call that LoLa provides
| xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-lola-assumptions-kept[A deployment on LoLa keeps the assumptions of LoLa that the binding names for the integrator]; xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-timing-budgets[Timing claims rest on a supported configuration, and the item's budgets are the integrator's]

| `+NoApisFromImplementationNamespace+`
| Nothing in LoLa's implementation namespace is to be called directly, and no type in it is to be used directly. LoLa gives `+impl+` as an example of that namespace
| <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>>, "What the binding keeps itself": the assumption is about the binding's own calls

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-terms]]
== What LoLa, the LoLa binding, a loan and a sample are

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-terms~1",oft-title="What LoLa, the LoLa binding, a loan and a sample are",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*LoLa.*
LoLa is the shared-memory communication middleware that <<lola>> identifies.

*The LoLa binding.*
The LoLa binding is the binding that adapts the element to LoLa (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract]).
Where an item that names the LoLa binding speaks of what LoLa publishes, provides or does, it speaks of the revision and the configuration of LoLa that the binding is built against, which the binding's qualification record states (xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).

*A sample.*
A _sample_ is the storage of one value of an event in LoLa's shared memory.
A producer obtains it from LoLa, writes it and sends it, and LoLa then provides it to each consumer that subscribes to the event.
On the LoLa binding one sample carries one frame (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-layout,On LoLa, a frame is one envelope in one sample, in a fixed order>>).

*A loan.*
A _loan_ is a sample for as long as a producer holds it in order to write it: from the moment LoLa provides its storage, which the producer holds through LoLa's `+AllocateePtr+`, until the binding sends it or gives it back.

*A received sample.*
A consumer holds a received sample through LoLa's `+SamplePtr+`, from the moment LoLa provides it until the binding has returned it to LoLa (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-sample-lifetime,On LoLa, no subscription ends while one of its samples is held>>).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-binding-lola-terms~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the LoLa binding
Verification:: review (each statement and allocation that uses a word that this item defines, read with this item)

*Rationale.*
The items of the LoLa binding speak of loans and samples, and these words tie them to LoLa's own types.
LoLa's assumptions of use speak of a producer that holds the storage of an event's sample through an `+AllocateePtr+`, and of a proxy event that provides each received sample through a `+SamplePtr+` (source: `+aou.trlc+` at the commit that <<lola>> names, the assumptions `+OneProducerOnlyOneAllocateePtr+`, `+SkeletonAliveWhileItsAllocateePtrBeingUsed+`, `+EventSubscriptionActiveWhileHoldingSamplePtr+` and `+ValidityOfPointerOnLoLaPointer+`).

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-profiles]]
== The LoLa binding carries native and serialized routes, each as itself

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-profiles~3",oft-title="The LoLa binding carries native and serialized routes, each as itself",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*Two kinds of route.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* be able to carry both kinds of route, and it *MUST* lend LoLa's storage for both:

* a _native_ route, on which a sender initializes its payload in a loan and a receiver reads the payload in the sample that holds it (xref:wires/native.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused]);
* a _serialized_ route, on which a wire encodes into a loan and decodes from the sample (xref:wires/serialized.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns]).

*Preparation.*
For each declared route the binding *MUST* compare what the route declares with the capability that the binding declares: the payload's bound and its alignment, the counts and the QoS settings (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting]; <<req-ssr-binding-lola-capacity,On LoLa, a route's counts are declared and its storage is established at setup>>).

* Where its capability serves all of it, the binding *MUST* prepare the route as it is declared.
What the element then admits is stated in xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-route-form[A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name].
* The heap class that the binding states for the route, and the element's comparison of it with what the route declares, are stated in xref:../basics/resources.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default].
* The binding *MUST* declare the class none for a route only where accepted evidence in its qualification record covers both the binding's and LoLa's runtime paths that the route uses, and shows that neither acquires or releases heap memory on them (xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).
That evidence covers the first locks and the lazy primitives of LoLa that those paths can reach.
Without that evidence the binding *MUST* declare allocation inside LoLa under a named permit.
The waiver that then admits the route is stated in xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-waiver-required[Middleware heap needs a named waiver].
A permit changes neither xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-heap[Binding code allocates nothing in runtime mode on a native path] nor the allocation that a serialized route declares and that setup admits, and it establishes no stronger guarantee (xref:../basics/resources.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name, and admitted or refused]).
* Where the route needs a capability that the binding or LoLa does not have, the binding *MUST* refuse the route in route preparation, naming the route and that capability.

*LoLa's first locks and lazy state.*
The first locks and the lazy primitives of LoLa that a runtime path of the binding can reach are taken and forced, on every route, as xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lazy-forced[Lazy state is forced, and threads are idle, before running] requires.

*What is a reason to refuse.*
A reason is always something that the route declares and that the binding, or LoLa, does not have.
The reasons are those that the items of the binding contract and the requirements that are specific to the LoLa binding state, those of <<lola-sample-layout,the LoLa physical envelope>> included.
Among them are:

* a payload bound or an alignment that LoLa's storage for the route does not hold, or a payload alignment that the binding cannot establish in every mapping of a sample (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-layout,On LoLa, a frame is one envelope in one sample, in a fixed order>>);
* a count or a QoS setting that the binding does not serve;
* a limit that follows from an assumption of LoLa (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>>);
* storage that the binding cannot establish (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-capacity,On LoLa, a route's counts are declared and its storage is established at setup>>), or cannot keep as lent storage has to be kept (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]);
* a writer's level below the level that the service records (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-integrity-level[A lending binding records and reports the writer's level]).

The element's own setup checks, and their reasons, are stated in xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-route-form[A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name].

*A setup operation that fails.*
An operation that route preparation needs from LoLa or from the operating system can fail for a cause that is no capability of the route.
The binding *MUST* report such a failure as a failure of setup, naming the route and the operation that failed, and *MUST NOT* report it as a capability that the route lacks (xref:runtime.adoc#req-ssr-mode-init-failure[Initialization failure]; xref:runtime.adoc#req-ssr-mode-bounded-startup[Setup never waits without a bound]).

*Excluded reasons.*
The binding *MUST NOT* admit, refuse or change a route because of the name of its wire or of its payload encoding.
It *MUST NOT* refuse a serialized route because its wire declares allocation, or because its decoded values own heap memory (xref:../basics/resources.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name, and admitted or refused]).

*Native routes.*

* The binding *MUST NOT* serve a route that is declared native by a serialized encoding, by a staged copy or by storage that LoLa did not lend.
* The binding *MUST NOT* report or count a copied or serialized route as a native one.

*A serialized route's allocation.*
The binding *MUST NOT* let what a serialized route allocates reach a native route on the same transport, or work that the two share (xref:../basics/resources.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default]).

*What each route got.*
What each route's report states is stated in xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-error-route-report[Each route reports what it got], and how a refusal is reported in xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-route-form[A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name] and xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader].
--

*Rationale.*
A deployment on shared memory mostly sends values as they lie in memory, and it also carries serialized messages on the same transport.

_Capability and declarations._
To be able to carry both kinds of route is the binding's capability.
Whether one declared route is carried depends on what that route asks: a payload that is larger than LoLa's storage for it cannot be carried, whichever wire wrote it.
A wire that allocates asks nothing of LoLa's storage that a binding could lack.

_Choice of wire._
Which wire a message uses is decided where the message is declared, and the same wire runs on another transport (xref:payload-paths.adoc#independent[a wire and a transport are chosen apart]).
A binding that admitted routes by the name of a wire would tie the two together.

_Genuine native routes._
A native route that was served by a copy would still deliver correct values, and nothing in a test of values would show the difference.
Its resource behaviour would differ, and a deployment that relies on a route being native relies on that behaviour.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-capacity]]
== On LoLa, a route's counts are declared and its storage is established at setup

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-capacity~2",oft-title="On LoLa, a route's counts are declared and its storage is established at setup",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*The counts.*
For each route that it prepares, the LoLa binding *MUST* declare to the element, each as what it is:

* on a transmit route, how many loans the route can hold at once, a loan whose lease safe code forgot included;
* on a receive route, how many samples the route can retain at once, a sample whose return is pending and one whose lease safe code forgot included;
* how many samples LoLa keeps for the route, and what happens to a new sample when they are all in use.

*What each count states.*
Each count is declared as xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting], "What a declaration says", requires.
For each count the binding *MUST* also state the setting or the storage of LoLa that the count is taken from.
It *MUST NOT* present one of the three counts as another.

*Established at setup.*
Before its transition returns, the binding *MUST* establish that the storage which LoLa provides for a route holds what the route declares, as an amount that the binding guarantees to be available to the route.
An upper limit does not meet what a route declares.
Where that storage does not hold it, or where the binding cannot establish it, the binding *MUST* refuse the route in route preparation, naming the route, the count and the values it found (xref:runtime.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-no-latent-refusal[Nothing is refused at runtime for a reason setup already knew]).

*A count that an assumption of LoLa limits.*
A limit that an assumption of use of LoLa sets on a count, such as the number of loans that a producer holds at once for one event, is declared and kept as <<req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions,The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does>> states.

*In runtime mode.*

* The binding *MUST NOT* obtain more storage from LoLa.
* The binding *MUST* return an error for a request beyond a route's count, and *MUST* decide it from its own count, without asking LoLa for the storage (xref:../basics/error_model.adoc#req-ssr-refuse-before-heap[Refuse before touching the heap]).
* What a full route does with a new frame is its declared overflow policy.
The binding honors that policy, or it refused the route at setup (xref:runtime.adoc#req-ssr-qos-overflow[Overflow acts as declared]; xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting]).
--

Verification:: test, review
* *test*
** A route that needs more than the binding declares, refused in route preparation by name.
** LoLa configured with less storage than a route declares, refused in route preparation with both values.
** A route whose declared need the binding can state only as an upper limit, and not as an amount guaranteed to be available: refused in route preparation.
** A route at each of its counts: one request more is a returned error, with no call into LoLa for the storage, no heap use and no panic. A loan that is given back, and a frame that is returned, make room again. One whose lease safe code forgot never does.
** Each declared count compared with what the route in fact holds, with the resource, the unit, the scope, the meaning and the setting or storage of LoLa that the binding states for it.
** A full route given a new frame under each overflow policy that the binding serves: the declared policy applied.
** A route whose declared overflow policy the binding cannot honor: refused at setup.
* *review*: how the binding establishes LoLa's storage for a route, and where each declared count is taken from, at source level.

*Rationale.*
A route that runs out of storage in runtime mode cannot get more there, so the storage is settled before the transition (xref:../basics/resources.adoc#req-ssr-mode-no-setup-at-runtime[Setup is impossible in runtime mode]).
The element computes its own storage from deployment data (xref:../basics/resources.adoc#req-ssr-memory-computable[Startup storage is computable before start]).
The storage that LoLa provides is LoLa's, and only the binding can compare it with what a route needs.

_Each count as what it is._
A number of loans, a number of retained samples and a number of samples that LoLa keeps are three figures, and a deployment sizes each of them (xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-capacity-sizing[Capacities are sized for the worst case]).
An amount that is guaranteed to be available and an upper limit are different promises.
A report that gave one for the other would let a deployment size against a number that means something else.

_Count values._
How many loans or samples a route needs is a deployment's choice, and what LoLa serves is a fact of the revision that the binding is built against.

_Exhaustion and an error from LoLa._
LoLa's assumptions of use call an error from `+Allocate()+` non-recoverable (<<lola-assumptions>>, `+CheckForAllocationError+`, its note).
So a route that is merely full is refused from the binding's own count, and the binding never learns it by asking LoLa for one loan more.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-foreign-boundary]]
== Nothing unwinds across the LoLa binding's boundary, and a termination path is a panic path

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-foreign-boundary~3",oft-title="Nothing unwinds across the LoLa binding's boundary, and a termination path is a panic path",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*The boundary.*
LoLa is {cpp} code, and the LoLa binding calls it, and is called back by it, across a language boundary.
The binding *MUST NOT* let a call across that boundary, in either direction, be left by unwinding:

* the binding's {cpp} code *MUST* catch a {cpp} exception that a call into LoLa raises, and return it as an error value;
* Rust code of the binding that LoLa calls back *MUST NOT* panic.

*Errors.*
The binding *MUST* make each error that crosses the boundary a fixed-size value.
The binding *MUST* return it as the refusal of the operation that met it, or count it on its route (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
It *MUST NOT* turn it into a panic, and *MUST NOT* drop it.
The binding *MUST* report a result of LoLa that it cannot classify as a failure, and never as the more favourable reading.

*Named results.*
The binding *MUST* give each refusal and each counter that a result of LoLa becomes a name that states its cause.
It *MUST* state in its qualification record which refusal or counter each result of LoLa that it can tell apart becomes (xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).

*Termination.*
A path on which LoLa, or the binding's own {cpp} code, ends the process is a panic path under xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-panic[No panic on any peer input] and xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do]: a call that ends the process, such as one to terminate, to abort or to exit, and an exception that nothing catches.
The LoLa binding has no accepted panic path: no requirement of the up-rust SEooC requirement family accepts one for it.
Each such path that a peer's input, death or restart can reach is a known failure of the binding.
Where a known failure is stated, and which routes strict mode refuses for it, are stated in xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures] and xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/strict-mode.adoc#req-ssr-strict-ack-revision[An acknowledgement names a deviation by ID and revision].

*Where it holds.*
The binding *MUST* state in its qualification record the builds of its {cpp} code, and the ways of loading it, for which the first two clauses are established (xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).
It *MUST* state them as target profiles, each with the operating system and the means by which LoLa's processes notify one another on it, the C library and the {cpp} standard library, the compilers and whether the {cpp} code is built with exceptions, the way the binding's {cpp} code is linked and loaded into the process, and the width of pointers and of atomics.
The binding *MUST NOT* claim either clause for a build, a way of loading or a target that the record does not state, and *MUST NOT* claim a target by the name of an operating system alone.
--

ID:: `+req~ssr-binding-lola-foreign-boundary~3+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the LoLa binding
Verification:: test, fault-injection, review
* *test*
** An exception injected behind each entry of the boundary that the binding uses: returned as the refusal of the operation that met it, or counted on its route.
** An error value of each kind that the boundary can return, reported under the name of its own cause.
** A result of LoLa that the binding cannot classify, injected behind the boundary: reported as a failure.
* *fault-injection*: malformed frames, and peers killed and restarted, with no termination of a process that stays up.
* *review*
** Each function of the boundary, at source level: what it catches, what it returns, and that nothing it calls back can unwind.
** The termination paths that the binding's calls into LoLa can reach, listed for the revision of LoLa that the binding is built against.
** The qualification record's target profiles, with the builds and the ways of loading of each, and its mapping of LoLa's results to named refusals and counters.

*Rationale.*
The rule of every binding speaks of a panic.
In place of a panic, a middleware written in {cpp} throws, or it ends the process.

_Unwinding._
An unwind from {cpp} code into Rust code through a function declared with a non-unwinding ABI, such as `+"C"+`, is undefined behaviour, and so is a Rust unwind into code that does not support unwinding (source: the Rust Reference, Rust 1.98.1, chapter https://doc.rust-lang.org/1.98.1/reference/panic.html[Panic], section Unwinding across FFI boundaries, rule panic.unwind.ffi.undefined).
Nothing is guaranteed when {cpp} code disposes of or rethrows a Rust panic: only ending the process, or catching the panic in the same Rust runtime, has a defined outcome (source: the same section, rule panic.unwind.ffi.dispose-panic).
LoLa's assumptions of use ask the same of each callback that is passed to LoLa: none is to throw (<<lola-assumptions>>, `+NoneTerminatingCallbacks+`).

_Termination._
A process that LoLa ends is as lost as one that panicked, and xref:peers.adoc#req-ssr-peer-fault-model[The requirements hold against a faulty peer] requires that a faulty peer be able to cause neither.
So a termination path that a peer's input, death or restart can reach is a known failure of the binding, never a limit that a deployment accepts.

_Builds and loading._
Whether an exception that LoLa raises reaches a handler in the binding's {cpp} code depends on how that code and LoLa were built and loaded.
An exception that reaches the outermost block of a function declared not to throw calls `+std::terminate+` before any outer handler is found, and a handler is found only in the thread that raised the exception (source: the {cpp} standard's working draft N4861, {cpp}20, [except.spec] and [except.handle]).
Code built without exception support can keep an exception from passing through it, and between shared libraries, catching an exception depends on how the {cpp} runtime is linked and on the visibility of the exception's type (source: the libstdc++ manual of GCC 15.2.0, chapter Exceptions, section Doing without; the GCC 15.2.0 manual, sections Options for Linking and Options for Code Generation Conventions).
So the binding's qualification record names the builds and the ways of loading for which it is established.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-sample-lifetime]]
== On LoLa, no subscription ends while one of its samples is held

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-sample-lifetime~1",oft-title="On LoLa, no subscription ends while one of its samples is held",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*The rule.*
While any sample that a LoLa subscription provided is still held, the LoLa binding *MUST NOT* end that subscription.
Unsubscribing the proxy event ends it, and so does destroying the proxy event or its proxy.

*Held.*
A sample is held from the moment LoLa provides it until the binding has returned it to LoLa.
That covers:

* a frame that the element or an application retains;
* a frame whose return is pending cleanup (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do]);
* a frame whose lease safe code forgot, which is held for the life of the process.

*Any sequence of calls.*
The binding *MUST* keep the rule for every sequence of safe calls, in any order and from any thread.
It *MUST NOT* rely on an application returning its frames first.

*In runtime mode.*
A subscription is a binding resource, which stays attached in runtime mode (xref:runtime.adoc#req-ssr-release-none[Nothing is released in runtime mode]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-binding-lola-sample-lifetime~1+`
ASIL:: B

Verification:: test, compile-fail, review
* *test*
** A frame held across every operation of the binding that an application can reach, with its subscription still active afterwards.
** A forgotten lease, with its subscription still active and its sample never reused.
* *compile-fail*, or *test* where the sequence compiles: in initialization, a sequence that would end a subscription while a frame is held: it does not compile, or it is refused.

*Rationale.*
LoLa's assumptions of use ask that a proxy event not be unsubscribed, and its proxy not destroyed, while a sample that it provided is still held, and LoLa's note gives the reason: unsubscribing invalidates the memory that the sample points to (<<lola-assumptions>>, `+EventSubscriptionActiveWhileHoldingSamplePtr+`, its description and its note).

A frame that the element retains on a native route is a view into that memory (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]).
If the subscription ended first, safe code would hold a view of invalid memory, which xref:../languages/rust.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-sound[Safe code cannot cause undefined behaviour] forbids.

LoLa addresses that assumption to its user, and the binding is that user.
An application can hold a frame for as long as it likes, and can forget one.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-callbacks]]
== What the LoLa binding gives LoLa stays valid for as long as LoLa can use it

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-callbacks~2",oft-title="What the LoLa binding gives LoLa stays valid for as long as LoLa can use it",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*The rule.*
The LoLa binding gives LoLa callables that LoLa invokes, and with them the addresses of what those callables use.
The binding *MUST* keep each of them valid until LoLa can no longer use it.

*A no-use point.*
LoLa can no longer use a callable from a _no-use point_: a point that LoLa's own published documentation names, for the revision that the binding is built against, as one after which LoLa neither invokes the callable nor reads it.

* Where LoLa's documentation names a no-use point for a callable, the binding *MUST* keep the callable, and what it uses, valid until that point at least.
* Where it names none, the binding *MUST* keep them valid until the process is gone.

*Excluded points.*

* A statement of how long a callable has to stay valid at least is not one.
It is a minimum, and the binding *MUST* keep it as well.
* What one revision of LoLa is seen to do, in a test or in its source, is not one.

The binding's memory safety *MUST NOT* rest on LoLa giving a callable up at any other point.

*Through exit.*
A process can end while threads of LoLa still run in it.
While any of them can still run, the binding *MUST* keep valid everything that it, or a callable that it invokes, can reach in the binding.
That holds from the moment an exit begins until the process is gone, and it *MUST NOT* depend on the order in which exit handlers and static destructors run.

*What a callable does.*
A callable that LoLa invokes is binding code on a runtime path.
It keeps the rules of that path: it does not unwind (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-foreign-boundary,Nothing unwinds across the LoLa binding's boundary, and a termination path is a panic path>>), and it acquires and releases no heap memory on a native path (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-heap[Binding code allocates nothing in runtime mode on a native path]).
The binding *MUST NOT* let a callable that LoLa invokes wait without a bound.
The waits without a finite logical deadline that xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-bounded[A binding bounds its own work and its waits] allows other binding work do not extend to such a callable.
--

* *test*

** Each callable's waits, at source level: none without a bound.

*Rationale.*
A callable that LoLa holds is a pointer into the binding that only LoLa decides when to follow.
If what it points to has ended, the next call uses invalid memory, and the application's safe code caused it (xref:../languages/rust.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-sound[Safe code cannot cause undefined behaviour]).

_The published minimum._
LoLa publishes that every callback passed to it is to be valid for as long as its proxy is alive (<<lola-assumptions>>, `+ValidCallbacksWhileProxyAlive+`).
That is a minimum: it says for how long the binding owes validity at least.
It does not say that LoLa stops invoking a callback when its proxy ends, or at any other point.
None of the assumptions listed on this page names such a point.
So, unless another published document of LoLa names one, the life of the process is the bound, and it is always long enough.

_Exit._
The element offers no shutdown, and a process in runtime mode leaves by exit, crash or kill (xref:runtime.adoc#req-ssr-mode-no-shutdown[No shutdown in runtime mode]).
An exit runs exit handlers and the destructors of objects with static storage duration, and destroys no object on any thread's stack, while other threads can still run (source: the {cpp} standard's working draft N4861, {cpp}20, [basic.start.term] and [support.start.term]; the standard library documentation, Rust 1.98.1, https://doc.rust-lang.org/1.98.1/std/process/fn.exit.html[`+std::process::exit+`], its description).
A thread of LoLa that then called into state the binding had already destroyed would corrupt the process while it still shares memory with its peers.
So what such a thread can reach outlives it, whatever the order of destruction.
On Unix an exit from Rust code calls the C library's `+exit+`, and the lock with which a copy of the Rust runtime orders its own exits orders neither an exit from C or {cpp} code nor one from another copy of the Rust runtime (source: the standard library documentation, Rust 1.98.1, https://doc.rust-lang.org/1.98.1/std/process/fn.exit.html#safe-interop-with-c-code[`+std::process::exit+`], section Safe interop with C code).
A program that calls `+exit+` more than once has undefined behaviour (source: the C standard's working draft N3096, C23, 7.24.4.4 The exit function).

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-assumptions]]
== The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-assumptions~2",oft-title="The LoLa binding keeps each of LoLa's assumptions of use, or names who does",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*Who keeps each assumption.*
LoLa publishes assumptions of use for whoever uses it, and the LoLa binding is such a user.
For each one that applies to the revision and the configuration of LoLa that the binding is built against, and for each part of it where it asks more than one thing, the LoLa binding *MUST* do one of two things:

* keep it itself;
* state in its README that the integrator keeps it, and what the integrator does for that (xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-lola-assumptions-kept[A deployment on LoLa keeps the assumptions of LoLa that the binding names for the integrator]).

The binding *MUST NOT* break an assumption that applies, and *MUST NOT* leave one, or a part of one, without a keeper.

*What the binding keeps itself.*
The binding *MUST* keep itself every assumption, and every part of one, that asks something of how its own code calls or uses LoLa, and every one that code of the element or of an application could otherwise break through safe calls.
It *MUST* keep it for every sequence of safe calls, in any order and from any thread, and *MUST NOT* leave it to the order in which an application calls it.

*What the binding names for the integrator.*
The binding *MUST NOT* state that the integrator keeps an assumption, or a part of one, unless it is a condition of the system or of its environment that the binding's code cannot establish, such as supervising time, configuring LoLa's storage, or reacting to a failure that the binding reports.
The binding *MUST NOT* pass an assumption on as a rule about how an application calls the element.

*An assumption that limits a route.*
Where keeping an assumption limits what a route can be served, the binding *MUST* declare the limit as its capability (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting]; <<req-ssr-binding-lola-capacity,On LoLa, a route's counts are declared and its storage is established at setup>>), and refuse what exceeds it:

* in route preparation, by name, where the route's declaration shows it;
* as a returned error otherwise (xref:../basics/error_model.adoc#req-ssr-refuse-before-heap[Refuse before touching the heap]).

The binding *MUST NOT* serve what exceeds the limit in another way, out of the caller's sight.

*Where a limit applies.*
A limit that follows from an assumption applies to routes on LoLa, for the revision and the configuration to which LoLa applies that assumption.
The binding *MUST* report it as a limit of its own, for that revision and that configuration.
The binding *MUST NOT* report it as a limit of the element, of another binding or of a route on another transport.

*An error that LoLa calls non-recoverable.*
Where LoLa publishes that an error means a failure that its user does not recover from, the binding *MUST* return it to the caller and count it (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
It *MUST NOT* retry it out of the caller's sight, and *MUST NOT* report it as a condition that passes, as a route that is briefly full, or as an accepted limit or deviation.

*The README.*
The binding *MUST* list in its README each assumption that applies, under LoLa's own name for it, with who keeps it, or each of its parts, and by what means (xref:../safety/assurance.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).
--

*Rationale.*
LoLa addresses its assumptions of use to whoever uses it, as conditions for using it safely.
The binding is that user, so whatever the binding passes on from LoLa to the element holds only while every one of them is kept.

_Who can keep one._
Some assumptions are about how LoLa's interface is called: how many loans a producer holds, or when a subscription ends.
An application reaches LoLa only through safe calls of the element, so only the binding can keep these, and it keeps them whatever the application does.
Others are about the system: that timing is supervised, or what the system does after a failure.
The binding cannot keep those, so it names them for the integrator.
One assumption can ask both: `+CheckForAllocationError+` asks that an error from `+Allocate()+` be checked, which the binding does, and that the system then go to a safe state, which the integrator arranges.
The integrator already reacts to what the element reports (xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-application-reacts[The application reacts to what the element reports]), and the timing budgets of the item, in ISO 26262's sense, are the integrator's (xref:../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-timing-budgets[Timing claims rest on a supported configuration, and the item's budgets are the integrator's]).

_What the binding passes on._
A rule that an application had to follow in order to keep LoLa safe would be a condition of memory safety that safe code could break.
So no assumption reaches an application as a rule: the binding enforces it, or the integrator arranges the system for it.

_Limits on a route._
An assumption can make LoLa serve less than a route asks: one loan at a time, for example (<<lola-assumptions>>, `+OneProducerOnlyOneAllocateePtr+`).
The binding then says so as its capability and refuses the rest.
The limit is LoLa's, at one revision and in one configuration.
The element has no such limit, and another binding, or another revision of LoLa, may serve more.

_An error from allocating._
LoLa's assumptions of use ask that an error from `+Allocate()+` be checked and that the system then go to a safe state, and LoLa's note calls such an error non-recoverable (<<lola-assumptions>>, `+CheckForAllocationError+`, its description and its note).
A binding that retried it, or reported it as a route that is briefly full, would keep the application from the reaction that LoLa asks for.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-layout]]
=== On LoLa, a frame is one envelope in one sample, in a fixed order

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-layout~2",oft-title="On LoLa, a frame is one envelope in one sample, in a fixed order",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*The envelope.*
The _envelope_ is the byte layout that this item states: the LoLa binding's envelope (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract]), in which it carries one frame in one sample.

*The order.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* place a frame in one sample, from the sample's first byte: the header, the source hint, the sink hint, the metadata, padding, and the payload, with nothing between them but that padding.

*The header.*
The header is 24 bytes.
The binding *MUST* write each field at the place that this table gives, as an unsigned integer, least significant byte first, with the identifier, the version and the reserved bytes as the table states them:

[cols="1,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Bytes | Width | Field

| 0 to 3 | 4 | The identifier of the envelope: the four bytes `55 4C 4F 4C`
| 4 | 1 | The version of the envelope: `02`
| 5 to 7 | 3 | Reserved: written as `00`
| 8 to 11 | 4 | The length of the metadata
| 12 to 15 | 4 | The length of the payload
| 16 to 19 | 4 | The offset of the payload, from the sample's first byte
| 20 to 21 | 2 | The length of the source hint
| 22 to 23 | 2 | The length of the sink hint
|===

*Where each part stands.*
The source hint starts at byte 24 and the sink hint at its end, and the metadata starts at the end of the sink hint.
The payload starts at the offset that the header gives, and the payload's length is the header's payload length.
The binding *MUST* write each length and the offset as the parts stand.

*The version.*
This item states version 2.
The binding *MUST* write `02`, and a sample with another version is no envelope of this layout.

*The payload's place.*
The binding *MUST* choose the payload's offset so that the payload starts at an address that is a multiple of the route's declared payload alignment in every mapping of the sample, for a frame with no payload and for a payload of zero bytes too: in the sender's process, and in each process that receives on the route.
That the address is such a multiple in the sender's mapping does not establish it for a receiver's.
The binding *MUST* establish it at setup, for the revision and the configuration of LoLa that it is built against.
Where the binding cannot establish it for a route, the route is refused in route preparation as xref:lola.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lola-profiles[The LoLa binding carries native and serialized routes, each as itself] states, with the alignment as the capability named, and the binding *MUST NOT* serve the route with a weaker alignment or by copying the payload to another place (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]).
What a receiver checks in its own mapping is stated in <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-checks,On LoLa, a sample that is not a well-formed envelope is refused before any of it is used>>.

*Padding, and what follows the payload.*
The bytes between the metadata's end and the payload's offset are padding.
They exist only so that the payload starts where its alignment needs it.
The bytes after the payload's end, to the end of the sample, are not part of the frame.
Padding and the bytes after the payload carry no meaning.
The binding *MUST NOT* rely on what they hold, in a check, in a comparison or in any other way.
--

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-checks]]
=== On LoLa, a sample that is not a well-formed envelope is refused before any of it is used

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-checks~3",oft-title="On LoLa, a sample that is not a well-formed envelope is refused before any of it is used",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*What is refused.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* refuse a received sample:

* that is shorter than the header;
* whose first four bytes are not the identifier, or whose version is not `02`;
* whose reserved bytes are not `00`;
* in which a length or the offset, added to where its part starts, overflows or ends past the end of the sample;
* whose metadata length is zero, or greater than the receiving route's declared limit for metadata;
* whose payload length is greater than the receiving route's declared limit for a payload;
* whose source hint is empty, or whose source hint or sink hint is not a routing hint that the binding can read as <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-routing-hint,On LoLa, the routing hint is untrusted, and the metadata decides>> states;
* whose payload offset is before the end of the metadata;
* whose payload does not start at an address that is a multiple of the receiving route's declared payload alignment, where the binding maps the sample.

*Before any of it is used.*
The binding *MUST* check every length and the offset with arithmetic that cannot overflow, before it forms any range from them.
The binding *MUST NOT* let any part of a sample that it refuses reach the element's checks, a wire or an application.

*What a refusal becomes.*
For each sample that it refuses, the binding *MUST* either return the refusal as the error of the call that met the sample, or count it exactly once on the receiving route.
The binding *MUST* name the cause in that error or that counter: a refused envelope (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
The binding *MUST* return each refused sample to LoLa as it returns a frame whose lease is dropped, so that a refused sample does not stay charged to the route (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do]).
A refused sample is peer input, and xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-panic[No panic on any peer input] holds for it.

*What the element checks.*
Whether a frame has a payload, and whether that agrees with the payload length that its header gives, is checked by the element, in its admission of the frame's metadata, after the checks of this item have passed (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata,On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery>>, "The element's admission").
This revision gives the element the refusal that revision 2 of this item gave the LoLa binding: of a sample from whose metadata the binding could not read whether the frame has a payload, and of one whose metadata said that the frame has no payload while its payload length was not zero.
The transfer takes effect, and a composition of an element and a LoLa binding that stand on different sides of it is refused, as <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata,On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery>> states in "Element and binding generations".

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-presence]]
=== On LoLa, an absent payload and a present empty payload stay apart

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-presence~2",oft-title="On LoLa, an absent payload and a present empty payload stay apart",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*Presence.*
Whether a frame has a payload is what its metadata says: a frame with a payload encoding has a payload, and a frame without one has none.
The payload length gives only the length.

*Where the binding takes it.*
The LoLa binding reads none of these facts from the metadata (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata,On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery>>).
When it sends a frame, it takes whether the frame has a payload, the payload's encoding and the payload's length from the element's request for the loan.
When it receives a frame, it takes whether the frame has a payload from the element's hand-back.

*An absent payload.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* write a payload length of zero for a frame that the element's request for the loan states has no payload, and *MUST* report a received frame that the element's hand-back states has no payload as a frame with no payload, never as a payload of length zero.

*A present empty payload.*
The binding *MUST* keep the payload encoding of a frame whose payload the element's request for the loan states is present and empty, and write a payload length of zero for it, and *MUST* report a received frame that the element's hand-back states has a present payload of length zero as a present payload of length zero.

*Bytes with no encoding.*
The binding *MUST* refuse, before it sends, payload bytes for a frame that the element's request for the loan states has no payload encoding.

*Replacement.*
This revision replaces revision 1 of this item for the LoLa binding: the binding takes the facts above from the element's request for the loan and from its hand-back, where revision 1 of this item had it read them from the frame's metadata.
It takes effect in the composition that <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata,On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery>> names in "Element and binding generations".
--

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-routing-hint]]
=== On LoLa, the routing hint is untrusted, and the metadata decides

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-routing-hint~2",oft-title="On LoLa, the routing hint is untrusted, and the metadata decides",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*What a hint holds.*
The source hint holds the frame's source, and the sink hint the frame's sink, each as the text that the protocol maps its URI to, without the scheme and its colon, in UTF-8: the authority where the URI has one, and the four path segments (xref:../basics/uri.adoc#uri-definition[Mapping to URI], `+dsn~uri-path-mapping~3+`).

*What the binding writes.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* write, for every frame, a source hint that is not empty, and *MUST* write an empty sink hint exactly when the frame has no sink.
It *MUST* write each path segment of a hint in one spelling: upper-case hexadecimal digits with no leading zero, and `0` for a value of zero.

*How the binding reads a hint.*
The binding *MUST* read a hint by parsing it as that text, and *MUST* read alike every spelling that the mapping accepts, lower-case hexadecimal digits and leading zeros included.
It *MUST NOT* decide what a hint names by comparing the hint's bytes with the bytes of another text.

*What a hint is for.*
The binding *MAY* use a hint to choose the physical queue or listener that a sample goes to, and to keep a sample that matches no current receive for a later one.
It *MUST NOT* use a hint for anything else, apart from the checks of <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-checks,On LoLa, a sample that is not a well-formed envelope is refused before any of it is used>>.

*Identity and delivery.*
A hint is no identity.
The binding *MUST NOT* deliver a frame, admit it to a route or count it as a route's own because of its hint.
The decoded metadata and the receive's source and sink filters decide what a frame is and which receive it reaches (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata,On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery>>).

*A hint that disagrees.*
The binding *MUST NOT* let a hint that disagrees with the metadata change anything that the metadata decides.
What the element does with a frame whose metadata does not match the filters of the receive that a hint brought it to is stated in <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata,On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery>>.
The binding *MUST* keep a sample that it retains on its hint for a later receive, or, where it discards it, count its loss exactly once on the route whose queue or listener holds it, under a cause that names the discard of a retained sample, and *MUST NOT* discard such a sample uncounted (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
That count records the loss of a sample that the binding retained, and does not count the frame as that route's own.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-routing-hint~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the LoLa binding
Verification:: test, fault-injection, review
* *test*
** Hints stated from the sample-layout items before the binding runs, compared byte for byte with what it writes: a source with an authority and one without, a sink, and no sink.
** Path segments whose values have a digit from A to F, whose values a fixed-width writer would pad with zeros, and whose value is zero: written in upper case, with no leading zero, and as `0`.
** A hint spelled with lower-case hexadecimal digits and with leading zeros: read as the same source or sink as its canonical spelling.
** A sample whose hint names the receiving route while its metadata names another source: not delivered to that route.
** A sample whose hint names another route while its metadata matches the receive: delivered by the metadata, kept for a later receive, or refused by the element and returned or counted once; never delivered to a receive whose filters its metadata does not match, and never lost uncounted.

* *fault-injection*: hints that are not URI text, and hints that name a source the route does not declare, sent by a faulty peer: refused or kept as above, with no delivery on the hint alone and no loss uncounted.

Any process that can write a sample can write its hint, so a hint can only narrow where the binding looks for a frame, never decide what the frame is.

_One spelling written, every spelling read._
The protocol's mapping lets one URI be spelled in more than one way.
One spelling for what the binding writes keeps two writers' hints for one source equal, and a reader that parses takes no spelling of one source for another source.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata]]
=== On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-metadata~3",oft-title="On LoLa, the metadata is the selected wire's, and is checked before delivery",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola,SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Whose bytes.*
The metadata is the metadata of the frame, in the metadata format of the wire that the route selects (xref:wires/README.adoc#req-ssr-wire-metadata-format[A wire states the format of its frame metadata, and decodes it]).
The envelope gives its place and its length, and the LoLa binding *MUST* carry it unchanged.

*What the binding reads.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* read nothing from the metadata.
When it sends a frame, it *MUST* take whether the frame has a payload, and the payload's length, from the element's request for the loan; when it receives a frame, it *MUST* take whether the frame has a payload from the element's hand-back (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-presence,On LoLa, an absent payload and a present empty payload stay apart>>).
The binding *MUST NOT* decide anything from the wire, the payload family, the layout, the source or the sink that the metadata names.
This revision retires, for sending and for receiving, the reading of whether a frame has a payload that revision 2 of this item let the LoLa binding make from the metadata.

*The hand-over.*
Once a received sample has passed the checks of <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-checks,On LoLa, a sample that is not a well-formed envelope is refused before any of it is used>>, the LoLa binding *MUST* hand the element the frame's metadata extent and the payload length that its header gives, and no byte of its payload.
Within the same receive step, the element *MUST* admit or refuse the frame as the clause "The element's admission" states, and *MUST* hand its verdict back to the binding, with whether the frame has a payload for a frame that it admits.
The binding *MUST* return each sample whose frame the element refuses to LoLa once, as it returns a frame whose lease is dropped (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do]), and *MUST NOT* count it: its one count is the element's.
The hand-over is not a delivery to an application.

*The element's admission.*
Before a frame reaches its route, the element *MUST* make these checks of it, in this order and once, and *MUST* refuse it at the first check that it fails:

. the selected wire decodes the frame's metadata in the format that the wire states (xref:wires/README.adoc#req-ssr-wire-metadata-format[A wire states the format of its frame metadata, and decodes it]); a frame whose metadata does not decode is refused metadata;
. the metadata holds each field that the format requires, each in its range, and names a payload encoding exactly where it says that the frame has a payload;
. the metadata names a payload encoding, or the payload length that the header gives is zero; a frame whose metadata names no payload encoding while that length is not zero is refused as `+payload_presence_extent_mismatch+`;
. the wire, the payload family and the metadata layout that the metadata names are those that the route admits, on every route;
. the metadata matches the source and sink filters of a receive of the route.

The element *MUST NOT* evaluate a receive's filters for a frame that an earlier check refused, and *MUST NOT* deliver a frame to a receive whose filters its metadata does not match.
For each frame that it refuses, the element *MUST* return the refusal or count it once, on the route whose receive step held the sample, under the cause that the refusing check names, as xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once] states.
A frame that the third check refuses is counted as `+payload_presence_extent_mismatch+` and under no other cause, also where a routing hint brought it to that receive step: it is not counted as matching no receive, and it is not kept for a later one (<<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-routing-hint,On LoLa, the routing hint is untrusted, and the metadata decides>>).
A frame with a present payload of length zero, encoding identifier `0` included, passes the second and the third checks; what its empty input means is decided later, by the wire of its encoding (xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked[A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding]).
The element *MUST* make these checks before any of the frame reaches a wire's decoding of the payload or an application (xref:wires/native.adoc#req-ssr-native-agreement[A native type is read only under its exactly agreed layout]; xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked[A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding]), and *MUST NOT* make them again for the frame when it is taken (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-qos-ttl[Expired messages are not processed]).
These duties of the element replace two duties of the LoLa binding: in revision 1 of this item, that a frame whose metadata does not decode, or whose identity is not the route's, reach neither a wire's decoding of the payload nor an application; and in revision 1 of <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-routing-hint,On LoLa, the routing hint is untrusted, and the metadata decides>>, that a frame reach a receive only once its metadata has been decoded and the receive's source and sink filters have been matched against it.

*Element and binding generations.*
The duties that this revision, revision 3 of <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-checks,On LoLa, a sample that is not a well-formed envelope is refused before any of it is used>> and revision 2 of <<req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-presence,On LoLa, an absent payload and a present empty payload stay apart>> move between the LoLa binding and the element take effect in a composition whose element and LoLa binding both declare a revision of the specification that holds these revisions or later ones (xref:../contract-generations.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect], "The declaration").
The element and the LoLa binding *MUST* each make a composition with a part of the other kind whose declared revision of the specification holds earlier revisions of these items either fail to build or be refused before runtime mode, naming that part.

The element holds what the route admits and the receive's filters, so the check of a frame's identity and filters is the element's.

Both parts of one process have to agree on which of them checks, so a composition across the transfer is stopped before it runs.

[[req-ssr-binding-lola-envelope-lifetime]]
=== On LoLa, a loan exposes only its payload, and a receive view lives within its sample

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-lola-envelope-lifetime~1",oft-title="On LoLa, a loan exposes only its payload, and a receive view lives within its sample",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyLola"]
--
*Transmit.*
The LoLa binding *MUST* fix the metadata and the payload's offset when it creates a loan, and write the header, the hints, the metadata and the padding itself.
What the binding gives a sender to write *MUST* be the payload's extent alone, and the binding *MUST NOT* let that extent move or change its size while the loan is held.

*Receive.*
The binding *MUST* make each view that it gives of a received payload cover exactly the payload's extent.
The binding *MUST NOT* let a view outlive the sample that holds it, and releasing the sample ends every view of it (xref:lola.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lola-sample-lifetime[On LoLa, no subscription ends while one of its samples is held]; xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]).
--

* *compile-fail*, or *test* where the use compiles: a view used after its sample is released: it does not compile, or it is refused.
