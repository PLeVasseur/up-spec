= Safety at the transport layer (uP-L1)
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, layout and tags: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].

This part is the safety counterpart of xref:../../up-l1/README.adoc[the Transport & Session Layer (uP-L1)].
It holds three things:

* what a transport and a wire guarantee;
* how shared memory is lent or copied between integrity levels;
* what a deployment provides for its transports.

== Whom this part binds

Where an item is filed says which layer it concerns.
It does not say that every transport implementation must meet it (xref:../README.adoc#filing[filing an item does not widen it]).

* *Written for one element.*
The items here were written for the up-rust SEooC (xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[its README]) and for the bindings and wires that work with it.
They are not protocol requirements of uP-L1, and none of them names a transport implementation of another SDK, or an SDK of another language.
* *Bound by statement and allocation.*
Each requirement binds the parts that its statement and its "Allocated to" line name (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the parts that it names]): such as every binding or those among them that it names, every wire, every serialized wire apart from the end-to-end protection wire, each wire of the fixed-layout XCDR2 profile, one binding alone (<<one-binding>>), or the element itself.
Its tags say which repositories cover it (xref:../README.adoc#tags[the tags]).
* *Assumptions of use.*
The five assumptions of use filed here are that element's: three about every transport, and two about one binding and its middleware, iceoryx2.
Whom they bind, and that they replace no requirement: xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-assumptions-bind[An assumption of use binds the integrator who claims the scope, and replaces no requirement].
* *The protocol chapter stays in force for what it covers*: xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect].

== Words used in this part

These words are used as the items use them.
The items give them their meaning, and this list only points to it.

* A *binding*, the *binding contract*, a binding's *middleware* and *binding code*: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract].
* A *wire*, the *wire contract*, the *native wire* and a *serialized wire*: xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract].
Wires are filed with this layer, beside the bindings that carry what they write.
* A *lending* or a *handoff* binding: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract].
* A *zero-copy* route and the *copy route*: xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader].
* A binding with *two-way shared state*: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-shared-state[A lending binding says which way its ports write shared state].
* A *native* or a *serialized* route: xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract], and xref:payload-paths.adoc#paths[the two kinds of route, side by side].

== What is specific to one binding

The duties of every binding name no middleware (xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding]), and neither do the assumptions about every transport (xref:deployment.adoc[what a deployment provides for its transports]).
What only one binding must meet, and what only a deployment on it provides, is stated with that binding, in items that are allocated to it alone.

[cols="2,3,5",options="header"]

|===

* *No item of its own is not an exemption.*
A binding without a page of its own is bound, in full, by each item of the binding contract that binds it (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the parts that it names]).
* *Evidence stays with its binding.*
A test that runs on one binding over its real middleware is evidence for that binding, and none for another binding.
* *Another middleware.*
A binding on another middleware is a binding as xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract] states, and it is bound the same way.
No item of this area names one.

== What stays with the element

Some items concern a transport or a wire and are still filed with the element, because they are rules of the element's own modes, memory and reports.
Each item binds the parts that its statement and its "Allocated to" line name, and some of these name a binding or a wire beside the element (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the parts that it names]).
A binding or wire author reads them in xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the element's part]:

* the heap class that every wire, binding and route declares, and the stronger resource guarantees a route may require (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name]);
* waivers for middleware allocation (xref:../seooc-up-rust/waivers.adoc#req-ssr-waiver-required[Middleware heap needs a named waiver]);
* QoS, TTL and deadlines of a route, which bind an L2 role's route and a carrier route alike (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade[QoS is honored or refused, never weakened]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-deadline[Deadlines are monitored]).
They rest on a binding's declared capability and arrival latency, and on its middleware's own event deadlines being off on an ASIL deployment;
* release, the release service and pending cleanup (xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else]);
* memory safety against any wire and any binding written in safe code (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-wire-untrusted[No wire can break the element's memory safety]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-binding-untrusted[No binding written in safe code can break the element's memory safety]), and the inventory of `+unsafe+` (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-inventory[Every `+unsafe+` is inventoried and justified]);
* the deferred end-to-end protection wire.
It is a wire, but it is the element's own safety mechanism and reaches RPC calls and Streamer hops too, so its items stay together in xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc[`e2e-wire.adoc`].
