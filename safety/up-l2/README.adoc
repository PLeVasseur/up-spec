= Safety at the communication layer (uP-L2)
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, layout and tags: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].

This part is the safety counterpart of xref:../../up-l2/README.adoc[the Communication Layer (uP-L2)] and of xref:../../up-l2/dispatchers/README.adoc[its dispatchers].
It holds two things:

* interaction: what the element guarantees for an RPC call and for the identity of a message;
* forwarding: what the Streamer guarantees when it carries messages between transports, and what a deployment provides to operate it.

== Whom the items bind

Where an item is filed says which layer it concerns (xref:../README.adoc#filing[filing and scope]).

* *One element.*
The items here were written for the up-rust SEooC (xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[its README]), whose parts include the Streamer's data plane.
They are not protocol requirements of uP-L2, and none of them names an SDK or a dispatcher of another language.
* *Requirements.*
Each requirement binds the parts that its statement and its "Allocated to" line name (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the parts that it names]): the element, the Streamer or the Streamer binary.
Its tags say which repositories cover it (xref:../README.adoc#tags[the tags]).
* *Assumptions of use.*
The two assumptions of use filed here are that element's, about operating its Streamer.
Whom they bind: xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-assumptions-bind[An assumption of use binds the integrator who claims the scope, and replaces no requirement].
* *The protocol chapter.*
The protocol chapter stays in force for what it covers (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect]).
* *The dispatcher chapter.*
For a Streamer in ASIL mode, xref:streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-dispatch-obligations[Undeliverable messages are counted, never sent as dead letters] differs from that chapter's rule for a message that cannot be delivered.
xref:streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-route-gate[Each route has a heap-free gate]; xref:streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-qos-per-leg[Both legs are checked, at setup and per frame] and xref:streamer.adoc#req-ssr-config-exact-addresses[ASIL Streamer routes name exact source addresses] each have a frame refused for a reason that the chapter's rule on when a message may be discarded does not name.
xref:streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-rpc-error-response[An RPC request that cannot be dispatched is answered, best effort] sets conditions on the error response to an RPC request, which that chapter makes the dispatcher's responsibility.

== Items filed with the element

Some items that concern this layer are filed with the element, because they are rules of its own modes, memory, build and reports.
In their statements and "Allocated to" lines, some of these name the Streamer, its binary, a binding or the asynchronous layer, alone or beside the element (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the parts that it names]).
They are in xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the element's part]:

* QoS, TTL and deadlines of a route (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc[`qos.adoc`]);
* refusals, errors and route reports (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc[`errors.adoc`]);
* the check of a role's resource ID against the protocol's ranges, made with the validation of deployment data (xref:../seooc-up-rust/deployment-data.adoc#req-ssr-config-resource-ranges[Resource IDs are checked at setup]);
* the asynchronous interface's contract (xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-async-seam[The asynchronous interface has a written, executor-independent contract]);
* the Streamer's crate and its build (xref:../seooc-up-rust/crate-isolation.adoc#req-ssr-crate-streamer-isolated[The ASIL Streamer is its own untaintable crate]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-build-panic-abort[The ASIL build aborts on panic and checks overflow]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-build-specified-functions-only[The ASIL build contains only specified functions]);
* the deferred end-to-end protection wire, with the hops a protected frame survives (xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc#req-ssr-e2e-survives-hops[Protection survives every allowed hop] in xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc[`e2e-wire.adoc`]).

A carrier route that crosses integrity levels upward is a transport-layer rule: xref:../up-l1/integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-carrier-crossing[Upward carrier crossings copy once, or are refused] in xref:../up-l1/integrity.adoc[integrity levels].
