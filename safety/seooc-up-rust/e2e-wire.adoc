= up-rust SEooC: The end-to-end protection wire
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions and tags: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].
Scope and element boundary: xref:README.adoc[the SEooC's README].

Whom an item binds: xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the parts that it names].
Using the wire is the integrator's choice (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).

*Deferred.*
The E2E wire is not part of the current delivery.
Its fourteen items below keep their IDs and revisions:

* the deferral implements, passes and withdraws none of them;
* it gives them no gate or coverage credit;
* no item outside this file depends on them for a current obligation.

The integrator's assumption of use confines the duties it states for this wire to a route that selects it (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
A system that needs end-to-end protection supplies and justifies its own, as that assumption requires.

*Design notes.*

* The restart design is the full version, with both sender kinds.
* An RPC route's responses are protected: the server answers with the request's own counter, the client accepts only an exact match, and the message type, the result and the caller's identity are all protected (<<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>).
* A request the wire refuses is answered, final or advisory, except a corrupt one (<<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>>), and a sliding window judges each reference's counters (<<req-ssr-e2e-replay-window,A sliding window judges a reference's counters>>).
* Setup makes no deadline check for this wire.
Declaring a deadline on a route that selects it is the integrator's duty (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
* The in-place variant's requirements are added when its design is recorded.

[[req-ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire]]
== The E2E wire wraps any wire and leaves its bytes alone

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire~4",oft-title="The E2E wire wraps any wire and leaves its bytes alone",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
`+E2e<W>+` *MUST* wrap any wire `+W+` for the same message type.

*The header.*
It *MUST* add a header, 32 bytes on a topic and 48 on an RPC route, in the formats of <<req-ssr-e2e-header-format,The E2E header has one documented format>>.
The header *MUST* carry a data ID, a counter, its sender's incarnation, the frame's length, and a CRC over every other header field and the inner payload.
A method header also carries the fields that <<req-ssr-e2e-header-format,The E2E header has one documented format>> adds.

*The inner wire.*
It *MUST* leave the inner wire's encoded bytes unchanged.

*What it states about itself.*

* It *MUST* state its own wire identity, and its own payload encoding from the private-use range.
* Its maximum encoded length *MUST* include the header, and it *MUST* be exact when the inner wire is.
* It *MUST NOT* add heap use of its own, and *MUST* state the inner wire's heap claim for each direction.

*Contracts.*
It *MUST* pass the sequenced wire contract, and the inner wire passes the ordinary wire contract on its own (xref:../up-l1/wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-heap-claim-tested[A heap-free claim passes the wire contract]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire~4+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, heap-window
* *test*
** The sequenced wire contract on the E2E wire over the XCDR2 wire and over a test wire.
** A control that the ordinary wire contract suite still refuses the E2E wire, with its existing message.
** A byte comparison showing the inner encoding unchanged inside the frame.
** The wire's stated identity, payload encoding, maximum encoded length and its exactness, and the inner wire's heap claim, stated for each direction.
** The XCDR2 wire and the test wire passing the ordinary wire contract suite.

*Rationale.*
Wrapping preserves the inner wire as a separate choice, and the wrapper can claim only the heap behavior and size guarantees that the combined encoding supports.

[[req-ssr-e2e-header-format]]
== The E2E header has one documented format

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-header-format~3",oft-title="The E2E header has one documented format",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
The E2E wire's headers *MUST* have two fixed formats, each with a 64-bit CRC, documented with the crate:

* a 32-byte data header for topics;
* a 48-byte method header for RPC routes, which adds a Source ID, a message type, a result and a status.

The record *MUST* cover:

* the field order, the field sizes and the byte order;
* the values of the method header's fields, a refusal answer's results and kinds among them;
* the CRC's polynomial, initial value, reflection and final XOR;
* known-answer vectors for each header and the CRC;
* the CRC's Hamming distance at the largest frame the wire admits, from which an integrator works out each route's residual error rate.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-header-format~3+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test (the known-answer vectors, encoded and decoded), review (the stated Hamming distance, against the CRC's published properties)

*Rationale.*
A fixed, documented header permits independent implementations to check the same protected bytes.
Undetected header corruption remains part of the residual error analysis.

* no `+alloc+`;
* no Cargo features;
* no dependency other than `+up-rust-core+`;
* no `+unsafe+` code;
* no 64-bit atomic type.

[[req-ssr-e2e-detects]]
== The E2E wire detects each fault

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-detects~6",oft-title="The E2E wire detects each fault",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Header, CRC and data ID first.*
Decoding through the E2E wire *MUST* first check each frame's header, its CRC and its data ID.

* A frame whose length field does not match its size, or whose CRC does not match *MUST* be refused as corrupt.
* A frame whose data ID was not declared is refused.
* Either refusal *MUST NOT* change any state.

*Method fields.*
A request's method fields *MUST* be checked before its counter, and a request that fails them *MUST* be refused as corrupt and *MUST NOT* change any state.

*The counter.*
On every route but a response route, the counter is then judged, for each declared sender:

* the first frame after the receiver starts *MUST* be delivered, and its incarnation and counter *MUST* start the reference;
* a frame of the reference's incarnation is judged by its counter against the highest one delivered, through the window of <<req-ssr-e2e-replay-window,A sliding window judges a reference's counters>>.
** Each incarnation's counter *MUST* start at 0, and never wraps (<<req-ssr-e2e-sender-state,E2E sending state is one counter per wire value>>).
** A greater counter is delivered, and the counters it skips *MUST* open one gap, counted at once.
** A counter that has arrived, inside the window and at or above the floor, is a repeat, and is refused.
** A smaller counter that has not arrived is delivered late on a request route, and refused as late on a topic, while it is inside the window and at or above its floor; otherwise it is refused as below the floor or out of the window.
** These frames *MUST NOT* be adopted;
* a frame of another incarnation is judged by <<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>>.

*After the checks.*

* A frame that passes these checks and then fails the inner wire's decode *MUST* have advanced the reference.
* Each refusal *MUST* name what failed, without heap and without panicking.
* A response is judged by <<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>.
* Which refused requests are answered, and how, is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>> and xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-undecodable-answered[An undecodable request is answered].

*Timeout.*
The E2E wire's timeout *MUST* be its route's deadline (xref:qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-deadline[Deadlines are monitored]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-detects~6+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, fault-injection, heap-window
* *test*
** After a receiver restart, each declared sender's first frame is delivered.
** A counter that crosses 2^32^ is accepted.
** A loss of more than 2^31^ frames within one incarnation is delivered, and counted.
** A frame that reveals a gap is delivered, and its gap counted once.
** A repeat is refused, and named.
** A reordered frame inside the window is delivered late on a request route and refused as late on a topic.
** A frame below the floor and one more than 63 counters back are refused, and named.
** A counter that has arrived and lies below the floor is refused as below the floor, not as a repeat.
** Corruption and an undeclared data ID are refused.
** A request whose method fields fail is refused as corrupt.
** Each header check refuses, and changes no state.
** A frame that passes and then fails the inner decode advances the reference, and a later copy of it is refused as a repeat.
** With the receiver's last counter at exactly `+resync_frames+`, a restart is not refused as a repeat.
** A silent sender on an E2E route with a deadline, counted as a deadline miss.
* *fault-injection*: a byte corrupted between hops; a torn copy on copy route.
ISO 26262:: 6-Annex D.2.4 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
Resynchronization is <<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>>.

Checking framing and integrity before changing receiver state keeps a corrupt header from becoming a valid sequence observation.
An undetected corruption remains a residual.

[[req-ssr-e2e-resync]]
== A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-resync~5",oft-title="A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Incarnations and declarations.*
Each frame *MUST* carry its sender's incarnation.

* For each declared sender, the deployment data on both sides says whether its starts increase.
By default, they are not declared increasing.
* For each declared sender, the receiver *MUST* keep the reference's incarnation and one run.
* For a sender declared increasing, it *MUST* also keep the newest incarnation adopted and one retired incarnation.

*The strict older-incarnation refusal.*
On a strict-mode ASIL RPC request route, the receiver *MUST* refuse every request whose incarnation is older than the newest it has adopted for that sender.

* Such a request *MUST NOT* start or extend a resynchronization run, become the reference, or move the newest adopted incarnation backwards.
* Each such refusal is counted by kind and answered under <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>>: final only with proof that this server instance never delivered that tag and never will, otherwise advisory.
* A request that fails the framing or integrity checks remains subject to their corrupt-request rule.
* A false declaration that locks a client out therefore produces counted refusal answers.
* The clients' persistent increasing starts are an initialization declaration under xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]; the receiver's rule is runtime E2E behavior selected during initialization.

*The run rule.*
The run rule applies subject to the strict older-incarnation refusal; in default mode, it applies without that refusal.

* *A sender declared increasing.* A frame of an incarnation newer than the newest adopted *MUST* be delivered at once: the reference's incarnation is retired, the reference moves to the frame, the run is cleared, and one restart is counted. A frame of the retired incarnation *MUST* be refused as stale. It *MUST NOT* start a run or be adopted.
* *Any other incarnation than the reference's.* Its frame *MUST* be refused as stale, unless it completes a run.
** The receiver keeps one run at a time.
** A frame of another incarnation than the run's *MUST* start a new run, at 0.
** A frame of the run's incarnation with a greater counter than the run's last frame *MUST* extend the run by one.
** A delivered frame of the reference's incarnation *MUST* end the run. So *MUST* the receiver's first frame, a restart, and an adoption.
** When the run reaches `+resync_frames+`, that frame *MUST* be adopted. It *MUST* be delivered, the reference *MUST* move to it, and one resync *MUST* be counted. For a sender declared increasing, the newest adopted and the retired incarnation *MUST* stay as they were.
** The frames refused before it *MUST NOT* be counted as lost.

*Restarts and arrival orders.*
While a sender's declaration is true:

* the restart of a sender declared increasing *MUST NOT* cost a refused frame;
* any other sender's restart *MUST* cost exactly `+resync_frames+` refused frames on a route that keeps order. They are counted from the last frame of the old incarnation to arrive, provided the new incarnation sends more than `+resync_frames+` frames;
* a live declared sender *MUST NOT* be refused for good because of the order in which its frames arrive. Displaced incarnations are exceptions: in default mode the retired incarnation of a sender declared increasing is always refused; on a strict-mode ASIL RPC request route every incarnation older than the newest adopted is always refused. An undetected corruption of a frame's header is not an arrival order: at the CRC's undetected-error rate, it can lock a sender out until it restarts (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).

*An undetected corruption of a frame's header.*
A counter corrupted upward makes the receiver refuse the sender's later frames, until the counter passes the corrupted value or the sender restarts.
For a sender declared increasing, an incarnation corrupted upward also retires the live sender, which is then refused as stale until it restarts, and the corrupted incarnation stays the newest adopted.

*The default-mode residual.*
In default mode, an incarnation older than the retired one can complete a run and become the reference.
Its late requests within their TTL can then execute, and a re-adoption can permit replay within the window (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).

*`+resync_frames+`.*
`+resync_frames+` is a `+u8+`, set in deployment data from 1 to 255, with 2 as its default; construction *MUST* check it.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-resync~5+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: fault-injection, test, heap-window
* *fault-injection*: a sender killed and restarted, with real processes on iceoryx2.
** Declared increasing, with a counter supplied at construction: no frame refused and one restart counted.
** Not declared increasing: exactly `+resync_frames+` frames refused and one resync counted.
* *test*
** A sender's clock reset at boot, so that its new start reads earlier than its old one: with the old incarnation's late frames arriving among the new one's frames, and with the receiver restarted while those late frames are in flight, so that they arrive after the new incarnation's first frame; for each kind of sender, with the outcome that the run rule gives.
** Three starts of one sender within one millisecond, ordered by their entropy and not by their start values, with the frames of one that died delayed; for each kind of sender, with the outcome that the run rule gives.
** Every order in which the frames in flight of two dead incarnations and of the live one can arrive, each incarnation's frames in their own order, from each state of the receiver's slot and under each order of the three starts, without and with a frame of each incarnation duplicated.

For a sender declared increasing whose declaration is false: the live incarnation refused as stale only once a frame of a newer incarnation has retired it while it was the reference, and from then on each of its frames refused as stale and counted; otherwise the first and the last assertion above hold.
** On a route that keeps order, while the sender's declaration is true, the restart of a sender declared increasing with no frame refused, and the restart of any other sender with exactly `+resync_frames+` frames refused, counted from the last frame of the old incarnation to arrive, where the new incarnation sends more than `+resync_frames+` frames.
** The sequenced wire contract's check that no arrival order refuses a live declared sender for good while its declaration is true.
** A counter raised under a matching CRC, and an incarnation raised under a matching CRC from a sender declared increasing, each with the refusals that follow it.
** With increasing starts A < B < C, adopt A, then B, then C and deliver delayed valid A requests with counters 0, 1 and 2, all within TTL, at `+resync_frames=2+`.
Strict mode refuses and counts each A request, answers each advisory because A is older than retired B and non-delivery cannot be proved, never adopts A, and still delivers a fresh C request.
Default mode refuses the first two A frames and adopts A on the third, retaining its disclosed residual.
** Strict retired-B requests and never-adopted incarnations between B and C get final answers only under the proof conditions of <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>>.
** `+resync_frames+` of 0, refused at construction.
* *heap-window*: the restart.

*Rationale.*
Per-hop peer identity cannot establish end-to-end restart order.
So the wire has its own incarnation rule, which trusts start order only where deployment declares it.

A persistent increasing source alone does not prevent old frames arriving after several restarts.
On a strict-mode ASIL RPC request route, the strict older-incarnation refusal keeps those frames from moving the reference backwards.
Refusal finality requires proof of non-delivery, and an older incarnation is no such proof.

[[req-ssr-e2e-replay-window]]
== A sliding window judges a reference's counters

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-replay-window~1",oft-title="A sliding window judges a reference's counters",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*The window.*
For each declared sender on a request route or a topic, the receiver *MUST* judge a frame of the reference's incarnation through a sliding window of 64 counters.

It *MUST* keep:

* the highest counter delivered;
* one bit for each of the 64 counters at and below it, set once that counter has arrived;
* a floor, the counter at which the incarnation became the reference.

When an incarnation becomes the reference, all its bits *MUST* be set.

*Judging a frame.*
A frame with counter c *MUST* be:

* above the highest: delivered, and the window slides; a counter that leaves the window without arriving is counted lost;
* inside the window and at or above the floor, with its bit set: a repeat, refused;
* inside the window and at or above the floor, with its bit clear: delivered late on a request route, and refused as late on a topic, and in both cases its bit is set, so that it is never counted lost;
* below the floor: refused, as below the floor; and more than 63 below the highest: refused, as out of the window.

*When the reference changes.*
When the reference changes, by a restart or an adoption, the old window's counters that have not arrived *MUST* be counted lost at that moment.

*Cost.*
The window *MUST* change only under the slot's try-lock, in constant time, without heap and without waiting.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-replay-window~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, miri, heap-window
* *test*
** Each case at its boundary: the window's two edges, the floor, a late request delivered and a late sample refused, a repeat inside the window.
** The window across a restart and an adoption, with its open counters counted lost.
* *miri*: the window's words under the slot's try-lock.

*Rationale.*
A bounded window can admit reordered requests once without accepting repeats.
A separate floor preserves the evidence needed for final refusal answers.

[[req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo]]
== An E2E response answers exactly its call

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-rpc-echo~2",oft-title="An E2E response answers exactly its call",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
On an RPC route, the E2E wire *MUST* use the method header of <<req-ssr-e2e-header-format,The E2E header has one documented format>>.

*Requests.*
A request *MUST* carry its client's data ID as its Source ID, message type request, result normal and status 0.
The server refuses one that does not, as corrupt, before judging its counter.

*Responses.*
A response *MUST* carry its server's data ID, echo its request's data ID as its Source ID, echo its request's counter and incarnation, and carry message type response, its result and its commstatus code inside its CRC.
It has no sequence of its own.

*What a client accepts.*
A client *MUST NOT* accept a response unless:

* its length, reserved bytes and CRC are intact;
* its type is response and its result agrees with its status;
* its data ID is a server the client declares;
* its Source ID is the client's own data ID;
* its incarnation is the client's own;
* its counter is the call's own, compared per call and not as a sequence over responses;
* its status is the response's commstatus.

A response with no E2E header is unprotected.

*What a client refuses.*
Otherwise the client *MUST* refuse the response, naming the cause: corrupt, undeclared data ID, misaddressed, stale, wrong call, status mismatch or unprotected.
A copy that another server answers is refused as an undeclared data ID when the client declares one server for the method.
Where a client declares several servers for one method, an answer from another declared server to a misaddressed copy is accepted (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).

*One response wire per method.*
The protection rests on one response wire per method, which the element *MUST* provide.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-rpc-echo~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire; the element, for one response wire per method
Verification:: test, heap-window
* *test*
** A request without its method fields, refused as corrupt before its counter is judged.
** Each acceptance condition and each refusal of a response, named.
** A response delivered to another client.
** A response to another call.
** A copy that another server answers.
** An answer from another declared server to a misaddressed copy, accepted where the client declares several servers for the method.
** A response with no E2E header.
** One response wire per method, which the element provides: on an E2E route, a response that names another payload encoding reaching no wire and refused, as xref:../up-l1/payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked[A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding] states.

*Rationale.*
A response sequence alone does not identify the outstanding call.
Echoing the protected request identity ties the answer to that call.

[[req-ssr-e2e-data-id]]
== One data ID per sender and route

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-data-id~4",oft-title="One data ID per sender and route",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Derivation.*
Each sender on a route *MUST* have its own data ID, derived the same way on both sides from deployment data, by one documented function of the sender's URI, the route's resource ID, the message type, and the route's fixed sink where it has one: a notification's destination, a request's method.

*Acceptance.*
A receiver *MUST* accept only the data IDs declared for its route's senders (an RPC server's declared clients; a topic's publisher), and *MUST* refuse and count a frame with any other.

*Construction.*

* A collision among one receiver's declared senders *MUST* be refused when the wire is constructed.
* The element *MUST* provide the derivation as a function, so that the integrator's tooling and both sides compute the same ID.
* A declared sender given as a wildcard has no data ID, and construction *MUST* refuse it.

*Responses.*
A response carries its server's data ID, and echoes its client's as its Source ID, so a response delivered to the wrong client is refused as misaddressed (<<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-data-id~4+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire; the element, for the derivation that it provides as a function
Verification:: test
* Two clients of one method.
* An undeclared sender.
* A second publisher on one topic.
* A collision in the declared list, refused at construction.
* A notification for another destination, refused.
* A response delivered to another client, refused as misaddressed.
* The function's test vectors.
* A wildcard in the declared list, refused at construction.

*Rationale.*
A route-only identity would merge different senders, and omitting the destination would admit a misaddressed frame.
So each data ID is derived from its sender and, where the route has one, from the route's fixed sink.

[[req-ssr-e2e-sender-state]]
== E2E sending state is one counter per wire value

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-sender-state~2",oft-title="E2E sending state is one counter per wire value",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*The E2E value.*
The E2E wire *MUST* keep its sending state in the E2E value: one counter and one incarnation per E2E value.

* The SDK never gives one E2E value to two roles.
* An `+E2e+` value *MUST NOT* be cloneable, so it cannot be a transport default.
* A publisher, a notifier, and an RPC client on its request route each send on one route, so the value's counter is that route's.

*The counter.*

* The counter *MUST* be 48 bits, and *MUST NOT* wrap: an encode at 2^48^ *MUST* be refused, by name.
* The counter *MUST* be kept in two 32-bit halves behind a try-lock.
* An encode that finds another encode of the same value in progress *MUST* be refused, by name, before the counter moves, so the receiver sees no lost frame.
* The lock sees a second sending thread only when two encodes overlap; the one-sender assumption of use still binds the integrator (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
* The counter *MUST* advance by one per encode, so an encode or send that fails, or a send refused and then retried, shows at the receiver as one lost frame.

*The incarnation.*

* The incarnation *MUST* be 80 bits: 48 bits of the sender's start value, read once at construction, followed by 32 bits of the platform's initialization entropy.
* The start value *MUST* be the platform's wall clock in milliseconds, or a value the integrator supplies at construction, as the sender's deployment data chooses.
* Construction *MUST* be refused, by name, if the start value or the entropy cannot be read.

*RPC servers.*
An RPC server's responses carry no sequence of their own: each echoes its request's counter and incarnation, so an RPC server's E2E value *MUST NOT* read a start value or entropy.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-sender-state~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: compile-fail, test, heap-window
* *compile-fail*: an `+E2e+` value as a transport default.
* *test*
** Two clients of one method raise no false gaps.
** A send refused and retried shows as one lost frame.
** Construction refused when the clock reads none, and when the entropy fails.
** An encode at 2^48^ refused, with the counter brought there through a test hook.
** Encodes across 2^31^ and 2^32^, with the counter brought near each through a test hook.
** A contended encode refused before the counter moves.
** A supplied start value used as given.

*Rationale.*
Sharing one sequence between independent senders would manufacture apparent loss and reordering.
Refusing overlapping encodes preserves the counter state.

[[req-ssr-e2e-receiver-state]]
== E2E receiving state is one fixed slot per declared sender

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-receiver-state~2",oft-title="E2E receiving state is one fixed slot per declared sender",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*One slot per data ID.*
The receiving state *MUST* be one fixed-size slot per declared data ID, in storage the caller provides at construction.
A slot *MUST* hold the state that <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> and <<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>> judge by, its window (<<req-ssr-e2e-replay-window,A sliding window judges a reference's counters>>) among it, and the sender's figures (<<req-ssr-e2e-figures,The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode>>).

*Updates.*
Updates *MUST* take a per-slot try-lock.
A frame that finds its slot held *MUST* be refused and counted as contended, and *MUST NOT* be waited on, so concurrent decoders cannot corrupt the state, and no thread blocks.

*Reads.*
The figures are read without the slot's lock.

*Route context.*
Route context *MUST NOT* reach the wire.

*RPC clients.*
An RPC client *MUST NOT* keep a receiving slot: it checks a response against its call's tag (<<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-receiver-state~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, miri, heap-window, review
* *test*
** A contended frame is refused and counted, never waited on.
** Frames of one route decoded concurrently, including from a push-binding test double that delivers them from two threads: a frame that finds its slot held refused and counted as contended, every other judged as <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> states, and the slot's state never corrupted.
** Reading the figures in a loop on another thread causes no refusal.
* *miri*: the try-lock under threads.
* *review*: one fixed-size slot per declared data ID, each in storage that the caller provides at construction, no route context reaching the wire, and an RPC client keeping no receiving slot.

*Rationale.*
Receiver state is larger than a single atomic word.
So it requires exclusive updates while its published figures remain readable without waiting for the state lock.

[[req-ssr-e2e-refusal-counted]]
== End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-refusal-counted~2",oft-title="End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Named.*
The element *MUST* report each end-to-end refusal as one fixed-size wire error variant that names what failed: corrupt, undeclared data ID, repeat, late, below the floor, out of window, stale, retired, older, contended, misaddressed, wrong call, status mismatch or unprotected.

*Counted once.*
It *MUST* count each one once, in one route counter, without heap:

* on a receive route, in place of `+refused+`;
* on an RPC client's response decode;
* when a response arrives.

An `+unprotected+` response *MUST* be counted in a route counter of its own, and not in the E2E counter.

*Calls and frames.*

* A call whose responses were all refused ends at its TTL, naming the last refusal.
* A refused frame *MUST* be released at once.
* Which refused requests are answered is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>>.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-refusal-counted~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test, heap-window
* *test*
** Each kind, on a subscriber's receive route and on a client's response decode, counted once in the E2E counter, with `+refused+` unchanged.
** An `+unprotected+` response counted in its own counter and not in the E2E counter.
** A call whose responses were all refused ending at its TTL, naming the last refusal.
** A refused request's frame released at once.

*Rationale.*
A response rejected before completion needs a visible cause, and a response with no E2E header is not corrupt protected traffic, so it has a counter of its own.

[[req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered]]
== A refused request is answered, final or advisory

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-refusal-answered~1",oft-title="A refused request is answered, final or advisory",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore,SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Answers to refusals.*
Every request that the E2E wire refuses *MUST* get one answer from the element, except a corrupt one: a request whose length, CRC, reserved bytes or method fields fail has nothing trustworthy to echo.

* A request whose data ID was not declared *MUST* be answered `+InvalidArgument+`.
* Every other refusal *MUST* be answered by the wire's refusal answer, in default mode and in strict mode alike.

*What the answer carries.*
Inside the method header and its CRC, the answer *MUST* carry:

* the message result 2, refused and final, or 3, refused and advisory;
* the status `+Aborted+`;
* the refusal's kind, in the first reserved byte: stale, repeat, out of window, below the floor, contended, retired or older.

*When an answer is final.*
An answer is final only when this server instance never delivered the request's tag and never will:

* in default mode, a request refused because its incarnation is not the reference, from a sender not declared increasing, whose counter is below `+resync_frames+` and whose incarnation is not the one the slot started with;
* in strict mode, also a request of the retired incarnation whose counter is above the highest that incarnation delivered, and one of an incarnation older than the reference, when none has been retired yet or when it is newer than the retired one.

Every other answer *MUST* be advisory.

*What the client does.*

* A client *MUST* complete its call at once on a final answer, as rejected with `+Aborted+`, naming the kind.
* It *MUST* give an advisory answer back, so the call stays open for its real response, and a call that ends at its TTL *MUST* name that kind.

*Sending and counting.*

* The element's own answers *MUST* be tried once, whatever the response route's overflow policy, and an answer the route cannot take *MUST* be dropped and counted.
* The server *MUST* count each refusal by kind, and each answer it cannot send.
* On an E2E route, the client's response queue *MUST* hold one more frame per call in flight.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-refusal-answered~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element; the E2E wire
Verification:: test, heap-window
* *test*
** Each kind answered, final or advisory as classified, in default mode and in strict mode.
** A corrupt request left unanswered.
** An undeclared data ID answered `+InvalidArgument+`.
** A final answer completing its call at once, and an advisory one given back.
** An answer the route cannot take, dropped and counted.
** The server's counts by kind.
** A client locked out by a false strict declaration told on every call, and counted at the server.

*Rationale.*
A final refusal is safe only when this server instance cannot also deliver the request.
Otherwise an advisory answer preserves the possibility of its real response.

[[req-ssr-e2e-figures]]
== The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-figures~2",oft-title="The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*The figures.*
The E2E wire *MUST* keep, for each declared sender and in its own fixed storage, figures that the application reads without taking the slot's lock, one atomic each, each saturating at 2^32^ − 1: lost, late and pending frames, gaps, restarts, resyncs, and refusals by kind.

* *Lost* counts each frame of the reference's incarnation that left the receiver's window without arriving, or was still open when the reference changed. So it lags the frame that reveals a gap by up to 63 frames; the gap itself is counted when it is revealed.
* *Late* counts a first copy that arrived inside the window after a greater counter.
* *Pending* is a gauge of the gaps the window holds open now, kept in an atomic of its own.
* At each adoption of an incarnation at counter c, the figures *MUST* add c to frames *not delivered before adoption*.
** This is exact at that incarnation's first adoption; at re-adoption it can over-count.
** For counters from 0 through c - 1, *never arrived before adoption* is c less the distinct frames of that incarnation that arrived in that prefix, including arrivals refused before adoption and counting duplicates only once.
** The wire *MUST* report that count as exact only when its retained distinct-arrival history proves it, subject to saturation.
** When the necessary history is incomplete, it *MUST* report unknown, explicitly distinct from an exact zero, and *MUST NOT* present a partial count or estimate as exact.
** A cumulative figure with any unknown contribution *MUST* remain marked incomplete; a later exact contribution does not restore completeness.
* For a receiver's first frame, the figures *MUST* record where the receiver joined: that frame's incarnation and counter.

*History and completeness.*

* The history and its completeness metadata *MUST* use bounded, initialization-provided storage and add no runtime allocation, freeing or waiting.
* Lost history, including history evicted on candidate changes or an arrival that contention prevents recording, *MUST NOT* produce a false exact result or stop recovery or otherwise permitted traffic.
* The count and its completeness indication *MUST* be published and read consistently: concurrent publication *MUST NOT* pair an incomplete count with an exact indication.
* Saturation *MUST NOT* be represented as an unsaturated exact count.
* These duties do not change the separate atomic lost, late and pending figures or the replay and recovery rules.

*Use and limits.*

* To react within the item's fault tolerant time interval, an application reads lost plus pending, or the gap count.
* The figures do not count the lost tail of an incarnation, or frames sent before the receiver started.
* A frame that the receiving route drops or expires after decode is counted by that route and *MUST NOT* be counted in the figures.
* A frame counted lost that then arrives, more than 63 counters late, *MUST* also be counted as refused.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-figures~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, heap-window
* *test*
** A gap, counted at once, with its frames counted lost when they leave the window.
** A late frame inside the window, counted late and not lost.
** A frame more than 63 counters late, counted lost and refused.
** Pending, read on another thread while gaps open and close.
** Not-delivered-before-adoption raised by c at each adoption at counter c, re-adoptions included, and exact at an incarnation's first adoption.
** Never-arrived-before-adoption exact when retained distinct-arrival history proves it.
** Prefix-boundary arrivals and duplicates counted correctly.
** An evicted duplicate, interleaved candidate changes or unrecorded contended arrival yielding unknown when necessary history is incomplete.
** Unknown distinct from zero.
** Cumulative incompleteness retained after later exact contributions.
** Concurrent count/completeness publication.
** Recovery continuing after history loss.
** A join's incarnation and counter.
** A restart.
** A resync.
** Each figure saturating.
** A frame that the receiving route's drop-oldest policy evicts after decode, counted by that route under its cause and not in the figures.
** A frame that the receiving route expires after decode, counted by that route under its cause and not in the figures.

*Rationale.*
A frame missing from a sender's counters is a certain loss only once the window has passed it or the reference has changed, and its gap stays pending until then.
The route counters of one hop and the E2E figures, which cover the stretch from encode to decode, answer different questions.

Distinct arrivals outside retained history cannot always be distinguished from duplicates.
So an explicit unknown preserves the observation limit without stopping traffic or requiring unbounded storage.

Completeness and saturation are different limits.

[[req-ssr-e2e-survives-hops]]
